Go to market

Win a district, not a country

Phase 1 · Land

One cluster, 50 shops

Sangli and Kolhapur first, where the institute’s network reaches pharmacists directly. Free for the pilot cohort, in exchange for weekly counter observation.

We sell the counter, not the marketplace. Billing software is a daily habit; buying is monthly.

Phase 2 · Density

The distributors follow

Once 50 shops in one district order through us, their distributors join to keep the business. Supply arrives because demand is already standing there.

Chemist associations and pharmacist councils become the distribution channel, not advertising.

Phase 3 · Repeat

Clone the cluster

A district that works becomes a playbook: the same ten steps, a local pharmacist as champion, a two-week onboarding. Ten clusters is the year-three plan.

Only now does national marketing cost anything.

Marketplaces die of thin supply. We avoid that by being useful to a pharmacy on day one with no distributor signed up at all.

The strongest answer to “how do you solve the chicken and egg problem?” is that we do not have one — the shop system is valuable alone. Say that explicitly; it is the single most common objection to a B2B marketplace.
